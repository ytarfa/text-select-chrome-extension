Lighthouses

A lighthouse is a tower designed to emit light from a system of lamps and lenses, serving as a navigational aid for pilots at sea. Lighthouses mark dangerous coastlines, hazardous shoals and safe entries to harbours.

The Lighthouse of Alexandria, built in the third century BC, is the most famous early lighthouse. Modern lighthouse keepers are rare: most lighthouses are now automated, though a few lighthouse museums still welcome visitors every summer.

On a clear night, the beam of a large coastal light can be seen from more than forty kilometres away.
